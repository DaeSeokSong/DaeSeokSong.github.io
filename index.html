01 / CURRENT RESEARCH

Swappable modules

Can a trained network hand a functional part to another network without relearning everything? Two lines of work: a preliminary measured result, and a design in progress.

LINE 1 Measured · preliminary

Module swap & alignment

Swap a module between separately trained image classifiers. Naive swaps cost accuracy; an orthogonal Procrustes alignment, with no retraining, recovered 99.4–99.8% of that loss in three structures (CIFAR-10, single seed 42).

LINE 2 In design · no results yet

Expert Synapse

Keep a large pretrained vision transformer frozen and link experts on its blocks to a task head, so that a head trained on one backbone can attach to an unseen backbone without retraining. Stage 0 is under way; the main runs have not started.

The long-term aim is an AI system whose functional parts people can inspect, replace, and eventually remove. Today’s results cover swapping and alignment only: I have not yet measured whether removing a module suppresses an unwanted behavior.

The swap work runs on a test bed I call RevolverNet. It starts from the Plug-in modules idea in my 2024 KSRR paper (second author; the paper’s author statement lists data acquisition and interpretation, while I carried out the algorithm work end to end); the experiments here are independent work. The broader question grew out of re-reading Hinton’s GLOM and Forward-Forward work and asking what it would take for a learning system to be modular while staying cheap in memory and energy. I checked the derivations and architectural claims against the literature using an agent harness I built for that purpose, then implemented and trained the design myself.

The two lines meet in one place: the closed-form orthogonal Procrustes alignment from line 1 is one of the four controls that Expert Synapse is compared against.

99.4–99.8% of the performance lost under naive swaps was recovered by an orthogonal Procrustes alignment with no retraining, in each of three completed structures (99.41, 99.84 and 99.63% before rounding; CIFAR-10, single seed 42).

Two-module image classification; preliminary, unpublished independent work. Aligned swaps stay slightly below the intact models (by 0.3, 0.1 and 0.1 points). C scores lower in absolute terms because two held-out classes are trained to be rejected and count as errors, so accuracies are not comparable across structures, and I make no claim about detecting unknown inputs.

Not yet tested for this result: whether it holds across different datasets, architectures, or class assignments (follow-ups I have deferred while working on Expert Synapse), and when a new chamber and bullet should be added. None of these is a completed result.

The test bed is RevolverNet. A bullet is a detachable subnetwork trained to own one or more classes. A chamber is the slot that receives it. A trigger routes each input to the responsible bullet in the structures that have a router (B and C); structure A reads out hierarchically without one. The schematic shows the idea.

In each structure, four models shared the dataset, architecture, and role assignment (roles were assigned by me, not learned) and differed only in initialization (Kaiming, orthogonal, uniform, sparse; one fixed seed, 42). Every directed donor-to-recipient pair among the four was swapped. For each swap, a mean-centered orthogonal Procrustes transform was computed from the donor and recipient modules’ outputs on the same input images; transferred bullets were not retrained, and every accuracy is measured on the full test set.

Recovery is a share of what naive swapping lost, not of the original accuracy. Independence holds only along the initialization axis: the result shows that mismatched coordinates are one cause of swap failure, not that modules are compatible across datasets or architectures, and no ranking between structures is claimed. Every figure comes from one seed (42), so run-to-run variation was not measured. Aligning independently trained networks already has a literature (model stitching, re-basin, relative representations), so I do not claim the alignment result as new.

In design Expert Synapse has no results yet on performance, transplant, or emergence.

A large pretrained vision transformer stays frozen. At each backbone block I attach a small bundle of experts, and a task head gets its own entrance experts. Features are rewritten as similarities to a fixed set of anchor tokens (relative representations, Moschella et al., 2023), and the two sides are linked through “signatures”, meaning how each expert responds to those anchors. The link is computed once when a head is moved, not relearned. The aim is for a head trained on one backbone to attach to a different, unseen backbone without retraining.

Four controls sit in the same slot: (a) a learned adapter layer per backbone pair, (b) a closed-form orthogonal Procrustes translation, which is the alignment used in the swap result, (c) relative representations alone, and (d) experts alone. Success criteria and tolerances are written in the approved design document before the main runs, the test bed is public COCO 2017 (box, mask, and keypoint heads), and a negative outcome will be reported as one.

Status: stage 0 (head choice, per-block probes, sanity checks) is under way and the main runs have not started. The design follows two routes I closed after they missed their pre-set bars: a learned translator into a shared feature space, and a fixed “convention” encoder. I do not claim novelty over relative representations (Moschella et al., 2023) or closed-form Procrustes translation between models (Maiorca et al., 2023). I do not claim emergent modularity either: the anchor form and routing rules are imposed by design, and whether roles emerge from learning alone is untested.

02 / PRODUCTION WORK

Medical AI, end to end

I joined an early team and learned the parts that research prototypes usually leave out.

2022.11 — PRESENTCONNECTEVE

AI Engineer · Early Team Member

Model research, clinical product integration, on-premise deployment, and regulatory evidence across the same product life cycle.

Multi-task systems

Consolidated 10 production knee X-ray models into 2 uncertainty-aware, partial-label multi-task models and added a keypoint model (3 models in total). Against the current product engine on the same CPU server and bilateral films, stored weights are 10.97× smaller (multi-task consolidation plus a tiny backbone) and per-image wall-clock inference time is 1.74× shorter; the overall F1 on evaluation sets from three external institutions did not drop (0.016 higher; single seed). A custom data loader lets a batch train even when not every sample carries every task’s label.

Deployment

Rebuilt the optimization-to-serving line (ONNX, 16-bit inference, BentoML): relative to the same engine before optimization, engine size fell 47% and inference ran 3× faster, with a −0.06 percentage-point accuracy change on clinical comparison data. These were measured on the earlier 10-model engine, so they are reported separately from the consolidation figures above and are not multiplied with them.

Medical foundation models

Converted medical data records into train-ready formats with a preprocessing process and database, and built a standard PyTorch Lightning experiment pipeline over DINOv3 and Rad-DINO with selectable linear probing or LoRA adaptation.

Evidence and regulation

Led authoring of the AI technical documentation for CONNEVO KOA and took part in the full approval process: cleared in Korea, approved in the UAE, CE MDR certified, and granted by Singapore HSA; FDA process in progress. Also handled technical documentation and third-party testing for a deep-tech TIPS program.
